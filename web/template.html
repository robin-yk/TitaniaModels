Ti–O solid–gas thermodynamics

Calculate oxide phase stability, vacancy distribution within a particle, and isolated-vacancy loss during pretreatment.

Vacancy distribution

R600 during H₂ reduction and CO₂ reoxidation at 600 °C. The calculation follows oxygen vacancies and Ti³⁺ from the surface to the centre of a 900 nm rutile particle.

The measured gas-release rates set the surface source and sink. The depth-dependent transport barriers are assumed: 1.6 eV in the outer 30 nm and 2.2 eV inside. The calculation starts with no defects.

Tested transport cases

Each row uses the same R600 source and sink. “Completed” means that the calculation reached 600 s of reoxidation. A stopped calculation records its last computed time.

Method

Joint vacancy and polaron states

State a of cell d has energy E, vacancy count v, electron count n, and degeneracy g. Its population is x = C p. The cell partition function gives the state probabilities. Charge enters through 2v − n.

\[Z_d=\sum_a g_{da}\exp\left[-\frac{E_{da}+(2v_{da}-n_{da})e\phi_d-v_{da}\mu_V-n_{da}\mu_e}{k_BT}\right],\qquad p_{da}=\frac{g_{da}}{Z_d}\exp\left[-\frac{E_{da}+(2v_{da}-n_{da})e\phi_d-v_{da}\mu_V-n_{da}\mu_e}{k_BT}\right]\]

The free energy includes state energies, configurational entropy, and electrostatic energy. In the equations below, populations are particle counts and concentrations are number densities; displayed inventories are converted to µmol g⁻¹. The implementation uses the equivalent inventory-normalised units. The state energies use a surface vacancy offset Δ = −1.2 eV and vacancy–polaron binding B = 0.8 eV. These two values define the tested conditional case.

\[\mathcal F=\sum_{d,a}x_{da}E_{da}+k_BT\sum_{d,a}x_{da}\ln\frac{x_{da}}{C_dg_{da}}+\frac12\sum_\ell q_\ell\phi_\ell,\qquad q_\ell=e(2N_{V,\ell}-N_{e,\ell})\]

Transport and electrostatics

The outer atomic planes retain discrete site energies. Spherical shells describe the interior. The electron and surface-state populations relax at each transport step. Poisson's equation determines the potential with relative permittivity 64.

\[\frac{\partial c_V}{\partial t}=-\frac1{r^2}\frac{\partial(r^2J_V)}{\partial r},\qquad J_V=-\frac{D(r)c_V}{k_BT}\frac{\partial\mu_V}{\partial r},\qquad-\frac1{r^2}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(r^2\epsilon_0\epsilon_r\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial r}\right)=e(2c_V-c_e)\] \[D(r)=\frac{a_h^2\nu_0}{6}\exp\left[-\frac{E_m(r)}{k_BT}\right],\qquad a_h=0.3\,\mathrm{nm},\quad\nu_0=10^{13}\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}\]

The centre has zero flux. At the surface, each removed oxygen adds one vacancy and two electrons during reduction. Each CO molecule removes one vacancy and two electrons during reoxidation. The measured rate curves supply these boundary amounts. Reduction lasts 1800 s; reoxidation lasts 600 s.

A finite-volume Scharfetter–Gummel flux and backward Euler time step solve transport. The displayed case uses 320 interior cells, maximum reduction step 2.5 s, and maximum reoxidation step 0.05 s. The graphs show saved checkpoints.

Validation

The integrated reduction source gives 95.719 µmol O g⁻¹. The measured R600 inventory is 94 µmol O g⁻¹. The endpoint difference is 1.83%. After 600 s of reoxidation, 14.194 µmol O g⁻¹ of vacancies remain and 81.525 µmol O g⁻¹ of vacancies have been filled. Both histories use the same depth-dependent mobility.

The source snapshot, input rates, raw results, and checks are stored in pilot/joint_state_release. The previous equilibrium distribution calculations remain in the source history and data archive.

Method

Scope. C–H–O–N / Ti–O Gibbs equilibrium. Gas registry in this version: CO2, H2, CO, H2O, N2, O2. Hydrocarbon species (CH4), graphite, nitrides, and the rutile TiO2−x solution phase are not included in this version. The calculation is a closed, finite-inventory equilibrium between one gas charge and one solid charge, not a flow-reactor steady state. Every reported value is gibbs_min.

Gas-phase RWGS atlas

The atlas first removes the solid from the question. For a fresh H2/CO2 feed it solves the single equilibrium

CO2 + H2 ↔ CO + H2O

on a one-mole fresh-gas basis. Because the reaction has no net change in gas moles, this ideal-gas conversion ceiling is independent of total pressure. The separate gas–solid curve then uses the oxide mass, gas charge, pressure, and volume from the input panel. A difference between the two curves means oxygen exchange with the solid has entered the equilibrium result.

Finite gas–solid contact

The solver computes the minimum of the total Gibbs energy

G = Σgas ni [μ°i(T) + RT ln(yiP/P°)] + Σsolid mj μ°j(T)

subject to the elemental balance \(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{b}\) and \(\mathbf{z}\ge0\), where \(\mathbf{z}\) collects all gas and solid amounts. Gases form an ideal mixture; each condensed phase is a pure line compound at unit activity. The inputs defining b are the initial gas composition, the gas charge \(n_{\mathrm{g},0}=PV/(RT_{\mathrm{charge}})\), and the initial solid phase and mass. The equilibrium phase split is an output.

\[\min_{\mathbf{z}\ge0}G(\mathbf{z};T,P),\quad\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{b};;y_i=\frac{n_i}{\sum_{k\in\mathrm{gas}}n_k};;b_e=\sum_{i\in\mathrm{gas}}a_{ie}n_i+\sum_{j\in\mathrm{solid}}a_{je}m_j\]

Amounts are in mol; standard chemical potentials are per mole of the species or solid formula unit. \(a_{ie}\) is the number of atoms of element \(e\) in species \(i\). The gas constant and chemical potentials must use the same energy unit, and \(P/P^\circ\) is dimensionless.

Active set. Condensed phases are enumerated as candidates: every single phase and every phase pair (the phase rule allows two line compounds in an M–O system at fixed T and P; N2 adds no solid component). For each candidate the equilibrium conditions are

gas:  μi = Σe aieλe    active solids:  μ°j = tjλTi + ojλO

solved by Newton iteration in the element potentials λ with the gas in logarithmic coordinates. Condensed amounts follow linearly from the Ti and O balances through the combination O − ρhost·Ti, which removes the host oxygen symbolically; coexisting phases are resolved down to ~10−41 mol. A phase outside the candidate is not a variable and is exactly zero in the result.

Stability criterion. Each inactive phase carries a reduced cost rj = μ°j − tjλTi − ojλO, reported per formula unit and per mole of oxygen (defj = 2tj − oj; defj = 1 for each TiO2→Magnéli step). The problem is convex, so a feasible candidate with all rj ≥ 0 is the global minimum. Candidates are not ranked by total Gibbs energy, whose differences can fall below double-precision resolution. Reduced costs at zero within tolerance are reported as phase coexistence.

Phase stability and iteration

Element potentials enforce atom conservation. Differentiating the constrained Gibbs energy gives the gas equilibrium conditions above and the solid-phase reduced costs:

\[\mathcal{L}=G-\boldsymbol{\lambda}^{\mathsf T}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{z}-\mathbf{b});;r_j=\mu_j^\circ-t_j\lambda_{\mathrm{Ti}}-o_j\lambda_{\mathrm O};;m_j\ge0,\quad r_j\ge0,\quad m_jr_j=0\]

A phase present at equilibrium has zero reduced cost. A positive reduced cost means that adding an infinitesimal amount of that phase raises the constrained Gibbs energy. For reduced titanium oxides, the plotted oxygen-normalized value is

\[\Delta_j=2t_j-o_j,\qquad r_{j,\mathrm O}=\frac{r_j}{\Delta_j}\quad(\Delta_j>0)\]

Each candidate phase set is solved iteratively. With the balance and equilibrium equations collected in \(\mathbf{F}\), the Newton correction satisfies

\[\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{u}_k)\Delta\mathbf{u}_k=-\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{u}_k);;\mathbf{u}_{k+1}=\mathbf{u}_k+\alpha_k\Delta\mathbf{u}_k,\quad0<\alpha_k\le1\]

Here \(\mathbf{J}\) is the Jacobian, \(\mathbf{u}\) contains the solver coordinates, and \(\alpha_k\) controls the step length. The iteration stops when the equilibrium equations and atom balances meet the numerical tolerance; the remaining phases are then checked for stability.

Oxygen-free feeds. Under pure H2 or N2 the single-phase candidate leaves the gas with zero oxygen inventory, and the derivative of G toward forming a trace of O-bearing gas together with a reduced phase diverges. The solver therefore reports the two-phase buffer with a finite trace amount; under sealed N2 this trace is the equilibrium result. An O2 impurity at the ppm level is sufficient to suppress reduction, with a positive margin.

The oxygen-potential margin used by earlier versions of this site equals the reduced cost per mole of oxygen (the dual of the same minimisation); it is reported in the phase-stability checks below. Independent verification is provided by an 80-digit reference implementation and by the NIST–Waldner data comparison in the validation section below.

Method

The titration gives the total oxygen removed per sample. The model splits it into three populations,

nV,tot = niso + nassoc + nbelow

Removal is generated uniformly over the reduction time, \(g=n_{\mathrm{V,tot}}/t_{\mathrm{red}}\). A new vacancy joins the surface active region of capacity ns with probability \(f_{\mathrm{free}}=1-(n_{\mathrm{iso}}+n_{\mathrm{assoc}})/n_s\) and otherwise goes below it. Two isolated vacancies associate at second order.

\[g=\frac{n_{\mathrm{V,tot}}}{t_{\mathrm{red}}},\qquad q=n_{\mathrm{iso}}+n_{\mathrm{assoc}};;f_{\mathrm{free}}=1-\frac{q}{n_s}\]

Population balances

\[\frac{dn_{\mathrm{iso}}}{dt}=gf_{\mathrm{free}}-\frac{2k_{\mathrm{loss}}}{n_s}n_{\mathrm{iso}}^2;;\frac{dn_{\mathrm{assoc}}}{dt}=\frac{2k_{\mathrm{loss}}}{n_s}n_{\mathrm{iso}}^2;;\frac{dn_{\mathrm{below}}}{dt}=g(1-f_{\mathrm{free}})\]

All three populations start at zero. Associated vacancies are counted as vacancies, rather than as pairs, so association transfers equal amounts between the first two populations. Summing the balances gives

\[\frac{d}{dt}(n_{\mathrm{iso}}+n_{\mathrm{assoc}}+n_{\mathrm{below}})=g;;n_{\mathrm{iso}}(t)+n_{\mathrm{assoc}}(t)+n_{\mathrm{below}}(t)=gt\]

Reduction to one equation. With \(q=n_{\mathrm{iso}}+n_{\mathrm{assoc}}\) the loss term cancels from the sum:

dq/dt = g (1 − q/ns)  ⇒  q(t) = ns [1 − exp(−g t / ns)],  nbelow(t) = g t − q(t)

Thus \(f_{\mathrm{free}}=\exp(-gt/n_s)\), and a single scalar equation remains:

dniso/dt = g exp(−g t / ns) − (2 kloss / ns) niso²,  niso(0) = 0
kloss = νeff exp(−Eloss / kB T)
nassoc = q − niso

At \(t=t_{\mathrm{red}}\), the sum of the three populations equals the measured total inventory. Populations and capacity use µmol O g⁻¹, time uses s, \(g\) uses µmol O g⁻¹ s⁻¹, and \(k_{\mathrm{loss}}\) and \(\nu_{\mathrm{eff}}\) use s⁻¹. \(E_{\mathrm{loss}}\) and \(k_{\mathrm B}T\) use eV. The scalar equation goes to an implicit integrator (Radau in the package, Strang splitting in the browser), which stays stable wherever a parameter search tries a small Eloss with a large νeff.

Rate. The initial CO formation rate is taken proportional to niso with one coefficient for every sample; rates are compared as ratios normalised to R600.

rCO(sample) / rCO(R600) = niso(sample) / niso(R600)

Fit. Three parameters, ns (bounded between the bridging row and the bridging row plus the first subsurface oxygen layer, 13.6 to 67.8 µmol-O g⁻¹), Eloss and log10 νeff, against five rates. The rates span 86-fold, so the residual definition moves the fit: a log or relative residual recovers the reported parameters, a raw residual is dominated by one sample.

Inputs and limits. niso is inferred from the measured rates. The rates constrain niso; nassoc and nbelow follow from the assumed second-order loss law, uniform generation and constant capacity ns. Surface area is held constant through the 1000 °C treatment (sample BET values unavailable). The model contains no reconstruction, cluster geometry or shear structure. Eloss and log νeff are effective parameters of the net isolated-vacancy loss rate constant kloss.
